TechNexus is a companion app for *FIRST*® Robotics Competition teams. It shows the match schedule, queue
position and results for an event you pick, and gives your team a few tools for keeping track of its own
equipment. There is no account, no sign-up and no login. I don't collect your name, your email address or
anything else that identifies you, and there is no analytics or advertising code in the app at all. This
page explains the little that does happen, in plain language.

I'm Jerry. I'm based in Canada, I wrote TechNexus on my own, and I run the server it talks to.

## What's stored on your device

The app remembers three settings, using the standard preferences store your phone provides
(`NSUserDefaults` on iOS). They live on your device:

| Setting | What it is |
|---|---|
| Event | The *FIRST* event you're following, as an event key such as `demo1815`. Starts on a demo event |
| Team number | Your team number, used to highlight your matches. Starts empty, and you don't have to fill it in |
| Live Activity toggle | Whether Live Activities are on. iOS only |

Your team number is never sent anywhere. It's used only on your own device, to pick out which rows in the
schedule are yours.

Deleting the app deletes all three.

## What's sent to the server

The app fetches schedule and results data from `https://nexus.jerryxf.net`, a server I run. Each request
carries:

- **The event key**, as part of the address the app asks for — for example `/event/{eventKey}`. This is how
  the server knows which event's schedule to send back.
- **Battery and cycle records**, but only if your team uses the battery tracker. These are notes your team
  writes about its own batteries. They describe equipment, not people.
- **Ordinary web request information**: your IP address, which app version made the request, and the time.
  Every web request on the internet carries these; they can't be turned off.

The server never receives your team number, a device identifier, or an advertising ID. There isn't one to
send — the app doesn't ask for either.

## Third-party services

TechNexus doesn't hand your data to anyone for their own purposes. But the server is built on infrastructure
other companies operate, and it reads competition data from two public sources. Here's everyone in the chain:

| Service | What it does | What it can see |
|---|---|---|
| [Cloudflare](https://www.cloudflare.com/privacypolicy/) | Sits in front of my server as a CDN and proxy | Your IP address and standard request information, in its edge logs |
| [Google Cloud Run](https://cloud.google.com/terms/cloud-privacy-notice) (`us-east1`) | Runs the server itself | Request logs, including your IP address |
| [Neon](https://neon.com/privacy-policy) (Postgres, AWS `us-east-1`) | Stores the database | Battery and cycle records only |
| [frc.nexus](https://frc.nexus) | Where schedule and queue data comes from | **Nothing about you.** My server asks frc.nexus, not your phone |
| [The Blue Alliance](https://www.thebluealliance.com/privacy) | Where match results come from | **Nothing about you.** Same — my server asks, not your phone |

The last two are worth being clear about: your device never contacts frc.nexus or The Blue Alliance
directly, so they never see your IP address or learn that you exist. My server fetches from them and passes
the results on.

## What TechNexus doesn't do

This list is short and I intend to keep it that way.

- No analytics, no telemetry, no crash-reporting SDK — none of it, of any kind
- No advertising, no ad identifiers, no ad networks
- No third-party trackers
- No location access
- No access to your contacts, photos, camera, microphone or health data
- No selling or sharing your data with anyone, for any purpose
- No accounts, so there are no passwords or profiles to be breached
- No messaging or user-generated content shared between users

## Retention and deletion

Server logs at Cloudflare and Google Cloud Run are retained for operational and security purposes — keeping
the service running and spotting abuse. They aren't used to build a profile of you, and I don't try to
connect them to anyone.

Battery and cycle records stay in the database until your team deletes them, or until you email me and ask.
They're team equipment notes, so they're most useful kept across a season.

Settings on your device stay until you delete the app or reset them in the app. Deleting the app removes
them, and that's the fastest way to remove everything TechNexus knows.

## Children's privacy

*FIRST* Robotics Competition team members are usually 14 to 18, so I've assumed from the start that some
people using this app are minors.

The app is on a public app store and there's no way for me to verify anyone's age. What I can do instead is
make sure there's nothing to protect: TechNexus collects no personal information, has no accounts, has no
profiles, and gives users no way to contact each other or post anything visible to anyone else. There is no
mechanism in the app through which a child could disclose personal information — to me, or to another user.

The United States' COPPA rule covers children under 13. Whether or not it applies here, the app collects no
personal information from users of any age, so there's nothing to obtain parental consent for.

If that ever changes — team-to-team messaging is the kind of feature that would change it — this page will
be updated in the same release that ships the change, not afterwards.

## Your rights and how to contact me

I'm in Canada, so PIPEDA applies to me, and Quebec's Law 25 may as well. *FIRST* is international, so some
of you are covered by the GDPR in Europe or the CCPA in California. Those laws give you rights to see, correct
and delete personal information a service holds about you.

In practice there's very little to exercise them against, because I hold very little:

- **To delete your settings**, delete the app. That's immediate and doesn't involve me.
- **To delete your team's battery and cycle records**, email me and tell me the event and team, and I'll
  remove them.
- **For anything else** — a question, a correction, or a request to see what's stored — email me and I'll
  answer.

The address is **[me@jerryxf.net](mailto:me@jerryxf.net)**.

The server and database run in the United States (`us-east1` and `us-east-1`), so the request information
described above is processed there, not in Canada or the EU.
